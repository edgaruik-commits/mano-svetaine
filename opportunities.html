Where local production is still missing

Regions with strong demand, available raw material and little or no local expanded glass production.

01

United States

Currently served largely by imports, primarily from Canada and Europe, with high tariffs and long-distance logistics adding significant freight costs. Rapid adoption of green building standards and lightweight concrete creates a major opening for local production closer to key construction hubs.

02

Southeast Asia

Robust glass-recycling infrastructure and the world's fastest-growing construction demand for lightweight materials. Despite abundant raw material supply, local expanded glass production remains severely underdeveloped, forcing heavy reliance on long-haul imports.

03

India

Rapid growth in thermal insulation, energy-efficient infrastructure, and green construction. There are very few local fine-granulate producers to date, leaving a large domestic market dependent on expensive overseas supply.

04

Australia & New Zealand

Tightening sustainable-building codes and harsh climate requirements drive high demand for high-performance insulation. With little or no regional production, the market depends heavily on carbon-intensive, long-distance maritime imports.

Common pattern

Why these markets are open

Import dependence

Demand is covered by long-haul imports from a few producing countries, adding freight cost and lead time.

Trade barriers

Tariffs and trade restrictions make imported granulate more expensive than local production would be.

Raw material available

Glass-recycling streams exist locally, so the key input for production is already in place.

Regulatory pull

Energy efficiency codes and green building standards keep increasing demand for lightweight, non-combustible materials.
